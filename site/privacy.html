Privacy

MX Shooting Calculator · in effect from 21 November 2026

Your rifles, shotguns, bullets, targets, shot log, groups, target photos and settings are kept on your own devices. The app has no accounts, no analytics and no advertising, and it sells nothing about you. It only sends something elsewhere when you use a feature that needs it: the weather, sync, a Garmin watch, or updates on a computer.

What the app keeps on your device

Everything you enter: rifles, bullets, loads and velocity measurements, scopes, shotguns with their chokes, shells and pattern counts, targets, stands, the shot log, your groups and range sessions, and the calculator's settings, including the place you last looked up and your county. On a computer it is a database file in your user's app-data folder; in the browser it is the browser's storage for this site. Deleting the app's data, or the browser's data for the site, deletes it. Export backup in the menu saves a copy wherever you choose.

Card settings, including stands and named label designs with their fonts, colours, marker styles and print choices, stay on this device. They are not included in exported backups or sync copies.

Groups and target photos

When you start a group, you take a photo of the target or choose one; the browser opens the camera or the photo library through its usual file picker, so the app asks for no camera or photo permission. On a computer you open a photo or drop it on the window. The photo is turned upright and, unless you choose Keep original photos, made smaller; either way its metadata is taken out as it comes in, with the location and the camera's details in it. Only the time it was taken is read first, for the group's date.

Photos stay on the device they were brought in on: on a computer in a folder beside the database (mxsc-photos beside mxsc.sqlite), in the browser in the browser's storage for this site. They are not in exported backups or sync copies, unless you choose Export backup with photos: that saves one ZIP file, wherever you choose, with the backup and the photos your groups are marked on (as kept on the device, without their metadata). Importing that file puts the photos back on the device you import it on. The app sends it nowhere. What you mark on them is: the groups with their shots, scale and aim point, the range sessions (place, date and weather), the record of each photo a group is marked on (named by a code worked out from the photo, with its size, the time it was taken and its scale) and the rounds counted on a barrel go into your backups and, if you sync, into your sync copy, encrypted like the rest. Another device then shows the group's drawing and numbers, but not the photo. A photo no group is marked on (one you brought in but saved no group on, or whose groups you deleted) is deleted from the device the next time the app starts. How a group was framed while marking is kept on each device only.

Velocities you type, paste or read from a chronograph file are kept with the group. Picture (PNG) and the CSV exports save files on your device only: the group's drawing, or its photo, with its numbers, or your groups and shots as a spreadsheet.

Weather and your position

When you ask for the weather, the app sends the place name you typed, or your position, to Open-Meteo, which answers with the forecast. For the weather, your position is read from the device only when you press Weather here, Use my position or, for a range session, Fill in the weather, and only after you allow it. It is used for the weather and for today's shooting light, and is kept on your device; a session keeps the weather that came, not the position. Weather at a session's place sends only the place's name. Open-Meteo's own terms and privacy policy cover what it does with requests.

The shot log and where you shot

When you press Log this shot, the app keeps the shot with the hold it gave, the weather, the wind and the range, and what you add: the outcome and notes. For a shot at game you can also keep where you stood and the bearing to the animal, from which the app works out where the animal stood. Your position is read from the device only when you press Mark my position (once) or Guide me there (as you walk, until you press Stop or leave the shot log), and only after you allow it; the compass only when you press Take bearing or Guide me there. While guiding, your position is only shown on the screen, not kept.

The positions you mark for your shots, and the animals' spots, are kept on your device, in your sync copy if you sync (encrypted on your device before it goes to the sync server, or in the app's own folder of your Google Drive), and in the backups you export. They are never sent anywhere else: the app shows no map and asks no map service. Copy coordinates puts them on your clipboard, to paste where you choose.

Sync with a sync key

If you turn on sync with a key, your data is encrypted on your device (XChaCha20-Poly1305, with a key made from your sync key) before it is sent to the app's sync server, which runs on Cloudflare. The server stores only that encrypted copy, under a hash of the key; it never sees the key or anything readable, and keeps no accounts or names. A copy nobody has written to for two years is removed. To keep any one sender from flooding it, the server counts the requests from each IP address over the last minute; Cloudflare does the counting, and the address is not stored or logged. Delete the synced copy in the app's Sync window removes the data at once; a note that it was deleted stays for 30 days, so your other devices stop instead of starting it again.

Sync with Google Drive

If you choose to sync through Google Drive and sign in with Google, the app asks Google for one permission: its own folder in your Drive (drive.appdata), a hidden folder only this app can see. The app keeps one file there, mxsc-sync.json, holding your rifles, bullets, loads, scopes, shotguns with their shells and pattern counts, targets and shot log (with the positions you marked for your shots), groups and range sessions (without the photos), so your devices share them. It cannot see or change any other file in your Drive, and it doesn't ask for your name or e-mail address.

The sign-in passes through the app's sync server, because Google requires a secret that can't be kept in the app. The server hands the sign-in only to the device that started it, within ten minutes, and keeps nothing afterwards: one that no device picks up is deleted within half an hour. Later it only passes requests for a fresh access token on to Google, without storing them. The sign-in is kept on your device. Your data goes straight from your device to your Drive, not through the server.

The app's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. The data is used only to sync your own records between your own devices. It is not sold, not used for advertising, not shown to anyone else, and not used to train AI models.

To stop: Stop syncing here in the app's Sync window, or Delete the synced copy to remove the file's contents from Drive too. You can also remove the app's access at myaccount.google.com/connections.

Your rifles on a Garmin watch

If you pair a Garmin watch (Garmin watch in the app), the app works out a profile for each of your rifles and loads: its name, its scope's click value, your units, the ethical limit for the chosen target, the time it was made and the holds out to a range in a spread of temperatures and air pressures. The watch app shows a code, and you type it into the app. The profiles go through the app's sync server, encrypted on your device with a key only that watch has (the watch and the app each make a key pair and agree a key from them). While the app is open, that device sends them again when your rifles, loads or units change, and the watch picks them up when you open it. The device keeps its key, and the secret that lets it update the watch, in its own settings, not in your synced data or backups.

For a paired watch the server keeps the two public keys, hashes of the watch's and the app's secrets, the encrypted profiles, which version the watch says it has, and when the pairing was made and last used: never anything readable, though it can see how many profiles there are, how large they are and when they change. It deletes all of it as soon as it hears that you have forgotten the watch in the app or the phone on the watch (both keep trying until it does), or when neither has used it for 400 days. A pairing the watch doesn't take is deleted after ten minutes (within half an hour at most). The server counts requests per IP address the same way as for sync, without storing the address. When pairing, the keys aren't checked against each other, so a server set on deceiving could swap them; that's why the watch lists the rifles and asks before it pairs. After that, the watch takes profiles only from the app it paired with.

On the watch, the profiles, its key and the paired app's public key, and the last range, wind, temperature and pressure you set are kept in the watch app's own storage, and nowhere else. The watch reads the air pressure from its own barometer; it doesn't use or send your position. It reaches the server through Garmin Connect on your phone, as every Connect IQ app's web requests do; Garmin's own terms and privacy policy cover Garmin Connect and the watch.

Updates on a computer

The desktop app looks for a new version only if you turn on Check for updates when the app starts in its menu (it is off until you do), or press Check now. It then downloads the latest release's update information from GitHub, where the app's releases are kept. Nothing is sent with it: GitHub sees the request and your IP address, as with any download. If you choose Update now, the app downloads the new version from GitHub too, checks that it is signed by the app's maker, and installs it.

AI assistants

The optional tool for AI assistants (MCP) runs on your own computer and reads the desktop app's database there. What you then share with an assistant is between you and its provider.

Websites and downloads

The web version and this page are hosted on GitHub Pages, and the downloads on GitHub; GitHub may log requests as described in its privacy statement. The app and these pages use no cookies, trackers or third-party scripts.

Children

The app is not directed at children.
