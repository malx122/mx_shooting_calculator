MX Groups: plan

A shot-group analysis app built like MX Shooting Calculator: photograph a target, mark the holes, get honest statistics, and follow them across rifles, barrels, loads and range days. Desktop (Mac, Windows, Linux), web and phone from one Rust code base.

Working name "MX Groups". Draft plan, 21 Nov 2026. Based on downrangeprecision.com (all public pages read) and the MXSC repo at ~/dev/ballistic calculator (v0.10.1). Sketches use MXSC's own palette from crates/app/src/theme.rs. Numbers in sketches are illustrative.

1. What Downrange does

Downrange is an Android app (plus a Pro-only web app, no iPhone) for precision rifle shooters. Its whole loop:

AreaWhat it does
MarkingPhoto or blank grid. Scale by 2 points and a typed distance, or 4 corners of a known rectangle for angled photos. Optional aim point, tap each hole, hold to select, drag to nudge, pinch zoom, glove mode, cold-bore shot, several groups per photo. Bullet-sized markers once a calibre is set.
Group statisticsExtreme spread (centre and edge), mean radius, CEP-50, horizontal and vertical SD, dispersion, width × height, impact vs aim, 95% intervals from 5 shots, hit probability and R50/R90/R95, a "called flier" toggle that recomputes without a shot.
VelocityPaste a string or import LabRadar / MagnetoSpeed / generic CSV; pair readings to shots to see how much vertical the velocity explains.
Hole detectionOn-device model on Android (web has an older, weaker one). Works only on the zoomed area, suggests dashed rings you confirm.
RecordsRifles with several barrels; round counts for the action, each barrel and since clean; cleaning log; loads and separate load-development profiles; range sessions with weather; zero resets.
AnalysisTrend (size or velocity against charge, seating, temperature, pressure, barrel rounds), Zero check (pooled impact since last reset: holding or drifting), Compare (cards, best and worst, a ratio test of two mean radii), Composite (pool groups into one), Window (load development: the run of charge or seating steps where impact moves least).
DataOffline, no ads or tracking. JSON backup (no photos), CSV and PNG exports (Pro), optional Sync to an EU server with Google or an e-mailed code.
MoneyFree: unlimited groups, 3 rifles, 5 loads, basic trend. Pro subscription: the analyses, exports, unlimited records and the web app.

What it does not publish: any of its formulas (CEP, intervals, hit probability, flier criterion, the ratio test, the window tolerance). We will define and document ours.

2. What we build

The same loop, rebuilt in our own words, look and maths, and tied to what MXSC already knows about your rifle, scope and bullet. Five principles:

Results first

Numbers update from the second hole. The group's correction reads the way MXSC reads a hold: "2 clicks left, 1 click up", using the rifle's own scope.

Honest statistics

Every size comes with how sure we can be. Small groups say so plainly ("5 shots can't tell these apart; about 14 each could"). All formulas are written down in docs/.

Built for the bench

A magnifier above your finger, bullet-sized rings that snap, big targets for gloves, one hand, undo on everything, and keyboard and mouse on the Mac.

Same rules as MXSC

Offline, no account, no ads, no tracking, no personal data; data on your devices; sync is optional and encrypted on the device.

Closes the loop

Measured precision, muzzle velocity, temperature drift and zero feed back into the calculator, so the dope gets better with every range day.

Where we go further than Downrange

  • Perspective really corrected: a 4-corner photo is shown straightened, not just computed straight.
  • Re-scaling keeps your marks: shots are stored in photo pixels, so fixing the scale never clears them.
  • Printable MX targets with corner marks: scale, perspective and aim point found by themselves.
  • "Find new holes": photograph the same target after the next string and the app finds what changed.
  • Ballistics-aware velocity analysis: compares the vertical your velocity spread should give at that distance (from MXSC's solver) with what you got.
  • Powder temperature sensitivity worked out from your sessions and offered to the bullet record in MXSC.
  • Three-way zero verdict: holding, drifting, or "can't tell yet", and a one-tap correction to the rifle's zero.
  • Works on iPhone (through the web app, like MXSC), Mac, Windows, Linux and Android.
  • No paywall (suggested; see question 2).

3. Where it lives

MXSC today has rifles (with scope and click value), bullets (factory or handload), rifle loads (velocities by temperature, impact offset, zero, truing), targets, a shot log and chronograph import. It has no barrels, round counts, powder/charge/seating, range sessions, groups or photos. Whatever we choose, those are new. Three ways to place them:

OptionUpsidesDownsides
A. A "Groups" part of MXSC (recommended)
new crates groups-core and groups-vision; screens in crates/app/src/groups/		 Rifles, scopes and bullets entered once. Measured precision, velocity, temperature drift and zero flow straight into the calculator. One database, one backup format (v8), one sync, one release pipeline, one app to install on the phone. Least new plumbing. MXSC grows (about +0.5–1 MB of WebAssembly for image decoding). The phone tab bar must make room (Cards to the menu). Hunters who never shoot groups see one more tab (it could follow the "what do you use it for" choice).
B. Separate app, same workspace
"MX Groups", its own binary, sharing crates		 A focused app with its own name and icon. MXSC stays as it is. The maths crates are shared anyway. The store is wired to MXSC's record kinds (PersonalData, RecordKind, migrations), so it needs generalising, and the UI kit (theme, sheets, tiles, number fields, i18n, files, web storage) must be pulled out of crates/app. Rifles live in two databases unless sync bridges them. Two PWAs on the phone.
C. Separate repository Fully independent releases. Everything copied and drifting apart; no shared data at all. Not recommended.
Decided 21 Nov 2026: A, a Groups part of MXSC, built as separate crates so a standalone app (B) stays possible later at little cost. The sketches below show A.

7. Statistics: what we compute and how

All maths in a pure Rust crate with unit tests against published tables, documented in docs/group-statistics.md. Shots are points pi in millimetres on the target plane (after the scale and any straightening); angles use the group's distance. The model behind the intervals is the usual one for rifle groups: shots scatter round a centre with the same normal spread in both directions.

StatisticDefinitionNotes
Centrec = mean(p_i)Impact vs aim = c − aim. Shown as impact and as the correction to dial, in the scope's clicks.
Extreme spreadES = max |p_i − p_j|; e-e = ES + calibreKept because everybody uses it; labelled as the noisiest measure.
Mean radiusMR = mean |p_i − c|Uses every shot.
Radial σσ̂² = Σ|p_i − c|² / (2(n−1))The best single measure of precision; feeds CEP, R90, hit chance, comparisons and MXSC's "Your precision".
95% range of σ (and MR, CEP)2(n−1)σ̂²/σ² ~ χ²(2n−2)Exact under the model, for any n ≥ 2, no resampling noise. ES's range comes from σ's range and the expected ES/σ for n shots.
CEP, R90, R95R_p = σ̂·√(−2 ln(1−p)): CEP = 1.177σ̂, R90 = 2.146σ̂, R95 = 2.448σ̂If the group is clearly elliptical (SD H/V ratio), the Grubbs approximation is used instead and the card says so. The measured median radius is shown alongside.
SD H, SD V, width × heightsample SD (n−1) per axis; max − min per axisFor wind (H) versus velocity (V) questions.
Hit chanceProbability a shot lands in a chosen circle or rectangle, centred on aim (includes the offset) or on the group.Circle around the group centre: closed form; with offset or rectangle: numerical integration of the bivariate normal. Targets from MXSC's target list.
Flier checkLeave-one-out: for each shot, σ of the rest and that shot's distance from their centre; flag if it lies beyond the 99% radius of the rest and removing it cuts σ by ≥ 25%.Only suggests. The user calls the flier; the view without it is labelled.
Velocityavg, ES, SD (n−1); SD's 95% range from χ²(n−1)Readings outside 100–1500 m/s refused; "2,850" is never guessed.
Velocity × verticalleast squares: vertical_i = a + b·v_i; R² = share explainedShown next to the solver's expected b at that distance and muzzle velocity: b well below expected suggests positive compensation, b near zero with a large R² is chance. Needs 5 paired shots.
Compare two groupsF = σ̂_A² / σ̂_B² ~ F(2n_A−2, 2n_B−2)Gives the ratio's 95% range and the verdict: same (range inside 0.9–1.1), different (range excludes 1), can't tell yet; plus the shots per group needed to tell this ratio apart 80% of the time.
CompositeAll shots pooled after moving each group to a shared aim point (or its own centre if any lacks one).σ from the pooled residuals, so mixed group sizes count correctly.
Zero checkPer-shot mean impact (net of dialled offsets) since the last reset, with a 95% range that includes group-to-group variation.Holding: range inside ±tolerance (default 0.1 mil / 0.35 MOA). Drifting: range excludes zero and centre beyond tolerance. Otherwise: can't tell yet, with how many more groups would help.
Ladder windowRungs at the same value pooled; each rung's vertical impact with its standard error σ̂/√n; every run of ≥ 3 neighbours scored by (max − min vertical) ÷ pooled noise, then horizontal, then σ.Velocity flatness (m/s per grain) shown per run. The help text is candid: with 3-shot rungs most "flat spots" are noise; the error bars show it.
Temperature sensitivityavg velocity per session vs temperature: m/s per °CNeeds 3 sessions spread over ≥ 10 °C. Offered to the bullet record in MXSC.

8. Data model

New records follow MXSC's newer pattern: a table with id, a JSON data blob and updated_at, merged by time, with tombstones for deletions. Units in field names, always metric. Everything below goes into the documented backup format (version 8) with generated schema docs.

RecordMain fieldsNotes
Bullet (extended)Handloads gain recipe: powder, charge_gr, primer, brass, coal_mm, cbto_mm, notes.Needed for ladders and "make this my load". Factory bullets unchanged.
Barrel newrifle_id, name, length_mm, twist, maker, fitted_at, retired_at, rounds_before_loggingRifle gains fitted_barrel_id. Fitting a barrel offers to copy its length and twist into the rifle's ballistics.
Round log entry newrifle_id, barrel_id (or action only), rounds, at, note, group_id?Action, barrel and since-clean counts are derived, never stored.
Clean newrifle_id, barrel_id, at, note
Session newplace, started_at, temperature_c, pressure_hpa, humidity, wind (speed, clock), notesWeather from Open-Meteo on request. Shot-log entries can link to it too.
Photo newid = SHA-256 of the bytes, width, height, mime, taken_atBytes are kept outside SQLite (see section 9). Same photo imported twice is stored once.
Group newphoto_id or grid; calibration (two-point a, b, mm | four-corner p1–p4, width_mm, height_mm | MX target); aim points; shots [{x_px, y_px, cold_bore, called_flier, velocity_mps?}]; view (zoom, pan); rifle_id, barrel_id, bullet_id, ladder_id + rung value; distance_m; session_id; condition overrides; dialled_offset_mrad (x, y); include_in_zero; tags; notes; archivedShots stay in photo pixels so a new scale never loses them. Several groups can share one photo and calibration.
Ladder newrifle_id, bullet_id, variable (charge | seating), notes, promoted_bullet_id?Kept apart from zero and trend unless chosen.
Zero reset newrifle_id, barrel_id?, at
Analysis newkind (compare | composite | ladder), name, group_ids, settingsRebuilt from the current groups when opened; a composite is listed in the library.

What flows back into the calculator: pooled σ for a rifle + load replaces the hand-typed "Your precision" group (Inputs.group_cm); average velocity and SD become a VelocityPoint with ShotStats on the RifleLoad; temperature sensitivity from sessions; the zero check's correction becomes the load's impact_offset_mrad. Each one is offered with a button, never written silently.

9. Technical design

Crates

  • groups-core: geometry, 2-point and 4-point calibration (homography), all statistics and tests (χ², F, normal quantiles written in-house like ballistics-core, no dependencies), composites, zero, ladder, trend. Pure, fully unit-tested.
  • groups-vision P3: hole suggestions, MX target marks, finding new holes. Uses the image crate.
  • ballistics-store: migrations 11+, the new records, backup v8, photo store trait.
  • crates/app/src/groups/: library, marking canvas, details, save sheet, analyse pages; rifles.rs and bullets.rs gain barrels, round log and recipe.

Photos

  • On the web, SQLite runs on an in-memory copy (relaxed_idb.rs), so photos inside it would sit in memory and be rewritten on every save. They go in their own IndexedDB store, keyed by hash; on desktop, files in the app-data folder under photos/.
  • Import: decode, apply EXIF orientation, reduce once (see Q4), store JPEG. HEIC: the iPhone browser converts it; on the Mac, decoded with ImageIO.
  • Display: GPU textures capped at 4096 px (the WebGL limit on many phones); full size kept for detection.
  • Backup: JSON without photos (as now), plus "Backup with photos" as a .zip.

The marking canvas

  • An egui widget: textured mesh, zoom_delta pinch and ⌘/Ctrl-scroll zoom, two-finger or space-drag pan, as in trajectory3d.rs.
  • Straightening is free: the photo is drawn as a 16 × 16 mesh whose corners go through the homography, so the GPU does the warp.
  • Loupe: a second mesh sampling the area under the finger at 3×, drawn above it (flips sideways near an edge).
  • Overlays painted as vectors at true bullet size. Undo/redo as a command stack per group.
  • Prior art to borrow from: tools/hitzone-editor.html (calibration, drag, nudge, undo).

Platforms

  • Phone: the web app, installable, as now. "Take photo" uses <input type=file accept="image/*" capture="environment">, so no camera permission is asked.
  • Mac, Windows, Linux: native as now; file dialog (files.rs), drag and drop, paste an image (arboard).
  • Back button and unsaved-changes guard: reuse web::navigation.
  • Same tests at widths 1200 and 390 for every new page.

Exports

  • PNG of a group drawn on the CPU with tiny-skia (works on the web too) and the cards' fonts.
  • CSV per group (one row per group) and per shot (mm and mrad, from the aim point and the centre).
  • Printable MX targets through the cards' existing PDF path.

Hole suggestions (P3)

  • Scale and calibre give the hole's size in pixels, so the detector looks for exactly that: a Laplacian-of-Gaussian at that size on the zoomed area, local contrast against a ring around it, roundness, then pruning by distance.
  • Touching holes: blobs about twice the area are split into two circles.
  • Find new holes: align the second photo through the MX target marks, or by tapping the same two reference points again; new holes are what changed.
  • All results are dashed suggestions; ones near marked shots start unticked.

Sync (P3)

  • The sync server caps a copy at 1 MiB. Groups are small, but a few thousand would pass it: compress the copy and raise the cap.
  • Photos as separate encrypted blobs (same key, content-addressed), which needs blob storage behind the Cloudflare worker (R2). The Google Drive route stores them as separate appdata files.
  • Both are server changes that CLAUDE.md asks your agreement for (Q5).

Privacy rules kept

  • No new permissions: camera through the file input, position only on the weather button.
  • No analytics, no accounts, nothing readable on the server.
  • site/privacy.html updated in the same change as photo sync.

11. Risks

RiskWhat we do about it
Fingers are less precise than the numbers suggestLoupe, snap to the hole's centre, nudge pad, and testing on real phones in milestone 1 before anything else is built on top.
Phone browsers run out of memory or texture size with 12–48 MP photosReduce on import, photos outside SQLite, one full-size texture at a time, cached thumbnails.
iPhone Safari clears data of web apps that aren't installed after a week without useAsk the browser to keep the data (as MXSC does), nudge to "Add to Home Screen", remind about the zip backup.
Five-shot groups invite overconfident conclusionsA 95% range on every size, plain-language verdicts, and "shots you'd need" in Compare and Ladder.
Hole suggestions miss or invent holesSuggestions only, last in the plan; manual marking is complete without them.
Sync copy limit and photo sync need server workPhotos local plus zip backup until milestone 5.
Looking like a copy of DownrangeOwn name, text, icons, layout and statistics; MXSC's look throughout. Nothing taken from its site or app beyond the idea of the features.

12. Questions before we start

Needed before we start (all answered 21 Nov 2026)

  1. Part of MXSC, or a separate app? Decided: part of MXSC (option A).
  2. Free, like MXSC? Decided: everything free and open, as MXSC is.
  3. Name? Settled by question 1: the tab is "Groups" in MXSC.
  4. Photo size: Decided: reduce to 4000 px on the long side on import (about 1.5–2.5 MB), with a setting to keep originals.
  5. Photo sync: Decided: photos stay on the device with zip backups first; the server change (compressed copies, raised cap, encrypted photo blobs in R2) comes in milestone 5.
  6. Phones: Decided: MXSC's installable web app, as now; no store apps.

The rest (all answered 21 Nov 2026)

  1. Hole detection: Decided: manual marking with loupe and snap first; classic image-processing suggestions in milestone 4; no trained model.
  2. Printable MX targets Decided: yes, in A4, A3 and Letter.
  3. Chronograph: Decided: Garmin Xero, LabRadar and MagnetoSpeed (the three MXSC already reads).
  4. Round counting: Decided: a tick on the save sheet, on by default and remembered.
  5. Handload recipe Decided: the full recipe: powder, charge, primer, brass, COAL, CBTO and notes.
  6. Angle units: Decided: add cm per 100 m beside mil and MOA.
  7. Hunting extras: Decided: neither for now.
  8. A light "sun" theme Decided: build it now, for the whole app, in the overnight run.
  9. Electronic targets Decided: skip for now.
